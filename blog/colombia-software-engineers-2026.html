Colombia in 2026: A Practical Guide to Hiring Software Engineers

Colombia has gone from overlooked to oversubscribed in the space of five years. Here is what you need to know before you start hiring there.

AB
Ala B.
Co-Founder, Resourcee

Five years ago, Colombia was not on most US companies' radar for engineering talent. Today it is one of the most requested markets we work in. The reasons are straightforward: the tech ecosystem in Bogota and Medellin has matured rapidly, English proficiency among technical professionals is high, and the timezone is Eastern, which makes collaboration with US teams almost frictionless.

Here is what you actually need to know if you are planning to hire Colombian engineers in 2026.

The Two Cities You Need to Know

Colombia's tech talent is concentrated in two cities. Bogota is the larger market, with a well-established tech community, a growing startup ecosystem, and strong proximity to multinational companies that have set up operations there. Engineers from Bogota tend to have exposure to large codebases and production environments at scale.

Medellin has become something of a tech darling in the past decade, driven by heavy government and private investment in digital infrastructure. The city's engineering community is younger and more startup-native than Bogota's, with a culture of entrepreneurship that produces engineers who are comfortable with ambiguity and rapid iteration. It is also where Resourcee's Latin American operations are based, which gives us particular depth in the local talent networks.

Engineers from both cities are excellent. The practical difference is that Bogota has a larger talent pool with more depth in enterprise and large-scale engineering, while Medellin skews toward product-focused, startup-oriented engineers.

English Proficiency

Colombia consistently ranks among the top countries in Latin America for English proficiency among technical professionals. In 2025, surveys of software engineers with university degrees in Bogota and Medellin showed that over 70 percent reported strong English comprehension and over 50 percent reported strong conversational fluency.

That said, English proficiency is not uniform. A structured English fluency assessment as part of the interview process is essential. What you are looking for is not perfect accent or grammar. You are looking for the ability to participate in a real-time standup, give and receive code review feedback clearly, and ask questions when something is unclear. Most Colombian engineers who make it through a reputable vetting process meet that bar comfortably.

What Roles Are Strongest

Colombia has exceptional depth in backend engineering, full-stack development, and cloud infrastructure. Node.js, Python, Java, and Go engineers are plentiful. React and mobile development talent is strong and growing.

QA engineering is an area of particular strength in Colombia, with a growing community of senior QA engineers who have worked in regulated industries including fintech and health tech. If you need someone who understands both testing rigor and production engineering, Colombia is a very good market.

AI and machine learning talent is growing but still developing. Senior ML engineers with production experience are available but competitive to hire. The pipeline is strong at the junior level, driven by university programs that have invested significantly in data science and AI curricula over the past four years.

Compensation Expectations

Colombian engineers represent good value in the LATAM market. A senior software engineer in Bogota or Medellin typically earns between $45,000 and $68,000 per year in total compensation, depending on the role and seniority. DevOps and cloud infrastructure engineers command a slight premium, typically $52,000 to $75,000.

These figures are fully loaded and include the local benefits, social security contributions, and other statutory obligations that apply under Colombian labor law. When hiring through an Employer of Record model, these costs are typically bundled into a single monthly fee that is predictable and straightforward to manage.

The Hiring Process

Colombian engineers expect a professional hiring process. They are in demand, they know it, and they will evaluate you as an employer the same way you evaluate them as a candidate. A hiring process that moves slowly, communicates poorly, or fails to provide clear feedback will lose good candidates.

Best practices: provide a written role description before the first technical screen, give timely and specific feedback at each stage, and keep the total process to four stages or fewer. Colombian engineers who have worked with international companies before are familiar with technical assessments, system design interviews, and async coding challenges. These are all reasonable and expected.

What Makes Colombian Engineers Particularly Good

Beyond the mechanics, there is something worth saying about culture. Colombian engineers tend to be highly collaborative, communicative, and invested in the success of the team, not just the completion of their own tasks. They ask questions when they are uncertain, which is a quality that is harder to find than technical skill. And they are genuinely proud of the work they produce in a way that translates into code quality and craftsmanship.

In five years of placing Colombian engineers into US teams, the most common piece of feedback we receive is some version of: I did not expect it to feel this much like working with someone in the office. That is the case for excellent nearshoring generally, and it is particularly pronounced with the Colombian engineers we have worked with.
